# Searching for a strict valley

## Range where a valley is possible

For a tree on $n$ vertices, write $\gamma$ for its domination number and $\Gamma$ for its upper domination number, the maximum size of an inclusion-minimal dominating set. Beaton and Schoonhoven's Theorems 6 and 16 show that $(d_k)$ increases weakly through $L=\lfloor(n+2\gamma+1)/3\rfloor$ and decreases weakly starting at $U=\lfloor(n+2\Gamma)/3\rfloor$. A strict valley needs a ratio below one followed by a ratio above one, both within the sequence from $d_L$ through $d_U$. The consecutive ratios must therefore increase strictly somewhere. This requires $d_{k-1}d_{k+1}>d_k^2$ at some $L<k<U$. The sequence has positive coefficients from $\gamma$ through $n$, so these ratios are defined. [Beaton and Schoonhoven](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02288)

On a tree, $\Gamma$ equals the independence number $\alpha$. Here is a direct reason for this equality in any bipartite graph. A maximum independent set is maximal and hence is a minimal dominating set. In the other direction, let $D$ be a minimal dominating set, $I$ the isolated vertices of its induced subgraph, and $R=D\setminus I$. Each $r\in R$ requires an outside private neighbor, since $r$ itself has another neighbor in $D$. The private neighbors are distinct and no one is adjacent to $I$. The induced bipartite subgraph on $R$ and these $|R|$ private neighbors has an independent set of size at least $|R|$ by taking its larger bipartition side. Adding $I$ gives an independent set of size at least $|D|$.

The seeded search ranks proposals by the maximum relative log-concavity failure within this band, multiplied by the tree order. It reproduces the first three coefficients shown for each tree in Figures 6–8, using the one-indexed Prüfer words printed in the figures. The seed log records the full set of violating degrees for each published example. ## Joining supports

The two-factor search forms all unordered pairs with replacement from its library of distinct non-log-concave polynomials, subject to the total-order limit. It uses the first tree representative encountered for each polynomial. The product is realized by joining supports in the two trees. The domination numbers add from the polynomial identity. The independence numbers add too. Each maximal-size independent set in a factor can avoid the joining vertex by replacing it, if selected, with one adjacent leaf. Thus the extra edge imposes no loss in maximum independent-set size. The central-band checks describe the chosen representatives. Unimodality is a property of the product coefficients alone. ## Reduced catalogue

For a minimum-order counterexample with at most 39 vertices, the support-edge reduction proves that its supports form an independent set. Unless the tree is a star, deleting the leaves leaves a nonleaf core that is itself a tree. Its leaves must be support vertices because their original degree was at least two. All remaining choices are an independent set of support vertices containing the core leaves, with each chosen support receiving a positive count of new leaves. Other core vertices receive no leaves.

The search uses one representative of every core shape in the requested core-order range. It tries all eligible support sets and all positive compositions of the number of leaves. A strict valley requires a close pair of bare vertices by the separation theorem, so it skips colorings with no such pair. Taking the smaller rooted encoding over the one or two core centers deduplicates isomorphic decorated trees. The core is intrinsic to the original decorated tree, so isomorphism preserves its center set. Stars and trees with nonleaf cores of order two or three cannot pass both the independent-support and close-pair conditions. [Enumerator](reduced_catalogue.py)
